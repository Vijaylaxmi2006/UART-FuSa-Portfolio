Problem Statement, Quantified Design Inputs & Background Study

Need Statement (Implementation-Neutral)

Safety-critical embedded links require deterministic serial data transmission where single-bit channel corruption is detected in hardware without processor intervention. Before synthesizing an FPGA UART core, the team must construct and validate a low-speed, visually observable discrete hardware prototype capable of framing 8-bit parallel words into asynchronous serial packets, reconstructing them at the receiver, and flagging single-bit transmission errors immediately.

Background Study & Candidate Functional Requirements

  • TI SN74HC165 & SN74HC164 Datasheets: Verified active-low parallel load ($\overline{SH/LD}$), clock inhibit ($INH$), and cascading rules (`U2` SO pin 9 into `U1` SI pin 10) for >8-bit serial framing.
  • TI SN74HC280 9-Bit Parity Generator/Checker Datasheet: Confirmed 9-input XOR tree propagation delay ($t_{pd} pprox 21 ext{ ns}$ at $4.5 ext{ V}$) and complementary $\Sigma_{EVEN}$ / $\Sigma_{ODD}$ outputs.
  • Candidate FRs: (1) Accurate 11-bit serial packet generation; (2) Glitch-free parallel LED display at receiver; (3) 100% detection of single-bit injected faults.

Concept Alternatives & Weighted Pugh Selection Matrix

Rejected Option A

Microcontroller Firmware UART (LPC2148 / Arduino)

Implement UART framing and parity check in C firmware. Reason set aside: Hides shift-register hardware mechanics and fails the discrete hardware gate-level verification objective before FPGA RTL design.

Chosen Concept B

Discrete 74HC Shift Registers + 74HC280 Parity + 74HC373 Latch

NE555 astable clock driving cascaded 74HC165 PISO TX, 74HC164 SIPO RX with 74HC373 output latch, and 74HC280 hardware parity checker. Selected: Direct 1-to-1 structural mapping to FPGA RTL blocks.

Rejected Option C

Discrete XOR Gate Tree + 74HC74 D-Flip-Flops

Build 11-bit shift registers and 9-bit parity purely from individual 74HC74 flip-flops and 74HC86 XOR ICs. Reason set aside: Requires 11+ ICs, excessive breadboard wiring capacitance, and high fault risk.

Calculation Card 02 · LED Current Limiting (FR3)

R_LED = (V_OH - V_LED) / I_LED = (4.9V - 2.0V) / 330Ω = 8.79 mA

Using $330\ \Omega$ resistors (`R3–R10`) limits current per 74HC373 output pin to $8.79 ext{ mA}$, providing a $64.8\%$ safety margin below the $25 ext{ mA}$ absolute maximum pin current.

Calculation Card 03 · 74HC280 Parity Setup Margin (FR4)

t_pd(74HC280) = 21 ns << T_clk = 72,200,000 ns (13.85 Hz)

Propagation delay through the 9-bit parity tree is over six orders of magnitude faster than the serial bit period, guaranteeing zero setup-time violations.

Foundation Integration Map & Decision Cards

Decision Card #01 — Cascaded PISO/SIPO with Transparent Latch

Level: Verified
Decision
Cascade two 74HC165 ICs (`U1, U2`) for TX framing and buffer RX 74HC164 (`U4, U5`) with a 74HC373 octal latch (`U6`).
Options Considered
(1) Single 8-bit 74HC165 without start/stop framing — rejected (no asynchronous sync); (2) Unlatched 74HC164 LEDs — rejected (visible shift ripple).
FR Served
FR2 (Serial framing) and FR3 (Zero-ripple parallel recovery).
Foundation Course
Digital Circuits — Synchronous sequential shift registers, edge-triggered clocking, and level-sensitive D-latches.
Where Courses Meet
Computer Organization (UART Protocol) — Asynchronous start/stop framing bits require >8 shift stages, met by cascading `U2.SO` into `U1.SI`.
Predicted vs Measured
Predicted: Clean 8-bit display upon `LE` pulse. Measured: Zero LED flicker across 20 test bytes after adding `U6`.

Decision Card #02 — 74HC280 9-Bit Hardware Parity Checker

Level: Applied → Verified
Decision
Use a single 74HC280 9-bit parity generator/checker across `D0–D7` + Parity bit instead of discrete XOR gates.
Options Considered
(1) 8-gate 74HC86 XOR tree — rejected (3 ICs, 4x propagation skew); (2) Software parity check — rejected (violates hardware safety isolation).
FR Served
FR4 — 100% single-bit fault detection in hardware.
Foundation Course
Digital Circuits — Modulo-2 arithmetic ($P_{even} = D_0 \oplus D_1 \oplus \dots \oplus D_7$) and combinational hazard minimization.
Predicted vs Measured
Predicted: $t_{pd} pprox 21 ext{ ns}$ and immediate Fault LED activation on any single-bit flip. Measured: Confirmed on breadboard across all 8 bit positions.

Debugging & Failure Log (Appendix E)

Cycle Symptom Test Performed Root Cause Correction & Re-Measurement Learning
#1 Output LEDs `D1–D8` flickered/rippled wildly while bits shifted into `74HC164`. Observed `U4/U5` outputs during 11 clock pulses in Proteus v1 schematic. `74HC164` has no output storage register; shifting bits appear directly on LEDs. Added `U6` (`74HC373` transparent latch) before LEDs; re-tested with clean latched transitions. Digital Circuits: SIPO shift registers require an output latch for glitch-free parallel display.
#2 Random parity errors occurred on breadboard even with no fault injected. Probed `U2` (`74HC165`) unused inputs and `74HC280` pins with multimeter. Floating CMOS inputs picked up 50 Hz mains hum and switching noise. Tied all unused `74HC165` and `74HC280` inputs firmly to GND and added `0.1 µF` (`104`) bypass caps. Digital Circuits: CMOS inputs must never float; always terminate unused pins to a defined rail.
#3 Double-clocking occurred when toggling manual DIP/load switches. Captured switch transition edge on DSO showing ~4 ms contact bounce. Mechanical switch contact bounce generated multiple false clock edges. Added $10 ext{ k}\Omega$ pull-down + $100 ext{ nF}$ RC debouncing on control line; verified single clean edge. Electronic Circuits: Mechanical switches require hardware RC or Schmitt-trigger debouncing.
